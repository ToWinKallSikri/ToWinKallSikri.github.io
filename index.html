Dissertation

Assessing the security impact of changes to OT systems

MSc Cyber Security · Lancaster University · Supervised by Prof. Paul Smith

My dissertation develops a practical way to assess how proposed changes could affect the security of operational technology (OT) in critical infrastructure. A new sensor, remote connection or cloud service can introduce risk, even when it supports an important operational need.

The resulting CIE-Informed Change Impact Metric (CCIM) applies Cyber-Informed Engineering principles to each proposed change. It gives teams a consistent basis for deciding whether to proceed, look more closely, or redesign the change, while considering security, usability and cost together.

Mar 2026 - Aug 2026
How the framework works
  1. Describe the proposed change and the system it affects.
  2. Assess its potential consequences, existing protections, digital connections and dependencies.
  3. Combine the assessment into a score and compare it with a threshold to guide approval or further review.
22 scenarios

Documented incidents and published mitigations informed the example assessment set.

20,000 simulations

Monte Carlo simulations explored how scores varied for acceptable and unacceptable changes.

AUC ≈ 0.993

In the simulated dataset, ROC analysis showed strong separation between incident scenarios and mitigations.

Case studies and findings
  • Microgrid: A remote feedback connection was flagged for redesign under both tested risk profiles. A proposed interlock mitigation passed.
  • Water treatment: A cloud-based chlorine-dosing optimiser scored close to the approval threshold. A small change in its assessment changed the result, highlighting the need for careful review of borderline cases.
  • Comparison: The framework builds on standards-led reviews by adding a consistent, repeatable way to document decisions.
  • Limitations: The scores and weightings are illustrative. Expert validation and a larger set of examples are needed before the framework could be used in practice.

Embedded Firmware: 1-Wire Sensor Emulation

Embedded Systems / Computer Architecture

Wrote bare-metal C firmware for an ATmega328P (Arduino Uno, simulated in Wokwi) that reads a DHT22 sensor and presents the data through a virtual 1-Wire device. It interprets the Convert T [44h] command, runs a single conversion, stores values multiplied by 10 as integers (one decimal place) in a custom 64-bit scratchpad with a checksum, then returns to idle.

Built under a "minimum machine resources" brief: independent state machines communicating through global flags, all timing driven by a 1 ms Timer1 CTC interrupt, short ISRs to respect real-time constraints, and direct register I/O (DDRD, PORTD, PIND) with no Arduino functions or external libraries.
